# LunaNav 3D audit — production preview, 15 Nov 2026

Captured with the repository virtualenv and Playwright Edge, D3D11, GPU blocklist ignored. Desktop 1440×900 and phone 390×844, six chronological frames per scene/mode. `before/metrics-main.json` contains the main sequence; `before/metrics-extra.json` contains follow cameras and autopilot. Screenshot paths below use `{desktop,phone}` and `01`–`06`. Baselines are 4-second requestAnimationFrame counts, including the initial rendering cost, measured independently for each mode. A browser RAF count measures responsiveness, not GPU render throughput.

## Findings before source changes

* **Title:** the existing title, mission data, and model are readable on desktop. Retain the hierarchy; inspect phone wrapping. Evidence: `before/{desktop,phone}-film-title-01.png`–`06.png`.
* **Launch:** the tiny single-cone rocket is difficult to follow against a huge Earth. The path starts away from the Kennedy marker, the +Z look-at attitude follows velocity rather than launch acceleration, and the trajectory changes speed abruptly. Parking orbit and departure do not share a reliable attitude or clock. A constant arbitrary light position cannot represent the launch date. Evidence: `before/{desktop,phone}-film-launch-01.png`–`06.png`.
* **Window:** the calendar communicates computed flags honestly. Preserve the 2D scene. Evidence: `before/{desktop,phone}-film-window-01.png`–`06.png`.
* **Coast:** Earth and Moon are stationary in a Moon-fixed sketch; the craft runs a 16-second route loop while the film calls it a 4.5-day coast in six seconds. The nominal range readout is unrelated to the craft position. Arrival misses the moving-target explanation entirely. Evidence: `before/{desktop,phone}-film-coast-01.png`–`06.png`.
* **Descent:** the pilot begins at a route-loop offset, not burn start. At desktop frame 04 the main HUD reads about 11 km while the route HUD follows a different descent time. The lander stands radially upright even while braking horizontally. The camera-facing circular engine glow is not an engine plume. Evidence: `before/{desktop,phone}-film-descent-01.png`–`06.png`; route follow sequences `before/{desktop,phone}-route-{chase,pilot,site}-01.png`–`06.png`.
* **Touchdown / standalone landing:** the short plume is already better than the previous task, but its 900-intensity point light paints an enormous orange spotlight on grey regolith. Attitude tilts toward travel instead of against it. Terrain contact assumes a flat plane; the rough variant bounces metres and invents a 5 m/s impact. The 26-second approach has no time-compression label. Evidence: `before/{desktop,phone}-film-landing-01.png`–`06.png`, `before/{desktop,phone}-landing-01.png`–`06.png`.
* **Site:** the terrain image and metrics are useful. A hidden live rover keeps rendering and planning behind this 2D insert, reducing responsiveness unnecessarily. Evidence: `before/{desktop,phone}-film-site-01.png`–`06.png`.
* **Ellipse:** explicitly schematic and appropriately 2D. Preserve its distinction from a real map. The same hidden rover wastes frames here. Evidence: `before/{desktop,phone}-film-ellipse-01.png`–`06.png`.
* **Rover:** wheels are completely static; models translate as rigid ornaments. The cinematic camera changes direction every 3.4 seconds, lerping through the vehicle rather than making clean cuts. The body uses a terrain normal, but parking and lander contact do not. Neither driving nor walking has lander collision protection. VIPER is a Mars rover proxy at the wrong target height. Large vehicle environment reflections and high fill lighting look like a showroom. Evidence: `before/{desktop,phone}-film-rover-01.png`–`06.png`, `before/{desktop,phone}-rover-01.png`–`06.png`, `before/{desktop,phone}-autopilot-01.png`–`06.png`.
* **Walk:** torch/readout separation is good. Terrain clearance during smoothed camera movement and lander exclusion need enforcing. Keep real Earth altitude and horizon occlusion. Evidence: `before/{desktop,phone}-walk-01.png`–`06.png`.
* **Odds:** dot positions are explicitly schematic. Preserve the existing computed probabilities and shares; no invented mission outcomes. Evidence: `before/{desktop,phone}-film-odds-01.png`–`06.png`.
* **Verdict:** existing desktop composition is strong; preserve the numbers and inspect phone bounds. Evidence: `before/{desktop,phone}-film-verdict-01.png`–`06.png`.
* **Route / LRO / satellite:** frame-rate-dependent camera easing, per-frame vector allocations, and no phase-specific braking attitude. A loop restarts instantly from Earth after touchdown. The orbiter follows its orbit, but the workshop orbit allocator creates vectors every frame. Evidence: `before/{desktop,phone}-route-overview-01.png`–`06.png`, follow-camera paths above, and `before/{desktop,phone}-orbiter-01.png`–`06.png`.
* **Hero:** the Moon rolls beneath a fixed horizon and Earth spins much faster than a day, with no declared time compression. Huge warm Sun glow and blue fill imply atmospheric scattering in lunar vacuum. Evidence: `before/{desktop,phone}-hero-01.png`–`06.png`.
* **Surface HUD:** its right edge is anchored, but the long vehicle-spec paragraph expands the entire panel across much of the viewport. Restrict copy width, fit buttons, and retain a high stacking layer. Evidence: rover/walk frames above; `before/{desktop,phone}-hud-hits.json` records hit tests.

## Inspection boundaries

No downloads or new dependencies. Existing models, textures, science inputs, tests, and unrelated concurrent edits are retained. Earth/Moon transit and launch are illustrative profiles; neither should claim to solve a specific mission trajectory. Model mesh names and normalised bounds are recorded in `model-bounds.json`; `wheel-sideviews.png` shows why rigid fused meshes need a local wheel rig. Six screenshots alone cannot prove collision, wheel phase, pause continuity, or monotonic telemetry; these also need numerical checks.
